## 1. Starting Question

The starting question was not:

“Can we immediately prove new physics?”

The starting question was:

“After standard GR waveform structure is removed or referenced, is there coherent residual morphology that repeats across events and survives background comparison?”

This shifted the project away from ordinary single-event visual anomaly hunting and toward a residual-morphology candidate-validation framework.

---

## 2. Research Philosophy

PRISM treats the post-GR residual not merely as leftover noise, but as a structured analysis domain.

Core principles:

1. Look for morphology, not just scalar excess.
2. Compare paired detector behavior, especially H1/L1.
3. Separate exploratory search from locked validation.
4. Use background/time-slide comparison before claiming candidate significance.
5. Freeze rules before final validation.
6. Preserve claim boundary.
7. Package only the locked/reproducible core for external technical review.

## 8. PRISM 2.3: QTB Frozen-Input Holdout

### 8.1 What 2.3 Tried to Do

PRISM 2.3 introduced the R1 QTB rule and froze a holdout-style validation path.

QTB means quiet-to-burst.

### 8.2 Research Question

Can a quiet-to-burst residual-morphology rule be frozen and reproduced on holdout events?

### 8.3 Primary Holdout Events

Primary 2.3 holdout:

- GW150914
- GW170104

Diagnostic/reference:

- GW190521_074359

Catalog-extension candidate:

- GW170814

### 8.4 Rule Concept

The QTB rule used:

- quiet gate,
- burst gate,
- contrast gate,
- coherent burst score,
- valid-slide empirical background.

The broad scoring idea:

```text
score = coherent_burst * min_contrast * (1 + min_quiet_depth)
```

Background calibration:

```text
empirical_p_valid = (exceed_valid + 1) / (N_valid_slide + 1)
```
